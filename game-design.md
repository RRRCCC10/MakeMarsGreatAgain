# Make Mars Great Again — Game Design Document (GDD)

## 1. Game Overview
**Make Mars Great Again** is a survival and management simulation set on Mars. Players assume the role of an astronaut tasked with managing resources, building a sustainable space base, conducting scientific research, and establishing a thriving long-term settlement on the Red Planet.

---

## 2. Core Game Mechanics

### 2.1 Resources
- **Budget / Funds:** NASA mission funding used to purchase resources, machinery, and upgrades.
- **Oxygen:** Essential for habitat survival and astronaut suit life support.
- **Water:** Required for survival, crop cultivation, and habitat reserves.
- **Food:** Sustains the astronaut and is replenished via Earth shipments or local farming.
- **Energy:** Generated by solar panels and distributed via transformers to power base infrastructure.

### 2.2 Player & Suit Stats
- **Hunger & Thirst Rates:** Continuous consumption meters requiring regular maintenance.
- **Heat Resistance:** Protects against extreme Martian temperatures.
- **Oxygen Tank Capacity:** Volume of breathable air stored in the suit.
- **Backpack Capacity:** Storage limit for carrying provisions during exploration.
*Note: Heat resistance, oxygen tank capacity, and backpack capacity are upgradeable through suit modifications.*

### 2.3 Habitat & Facility Specifications

#### **Main Building**
- Initial landing dome and primary storage hub for food and water rations.
- Houses internal modules such as the Research Center.
- **Stats:** Required oxygen ($m^3$), storage limits, base temperature, and internal oxygen levels.
- **Temperature Mechanic:** Monitored via a dynamic climate system (scale 1 to 100; if optimal it stays low, but higher values cause the player's internal temperature to rise).

#### **Water Extractor (Drill)**
- Placed on designated, limited extraction spots.
- Available in multiple tiers determining hourly water extraction rates ($m^3/h$).
- Connects via pipes to greenhouses and Main Building storage.

#### **Oxygen Factory**
- Extracts and produces breathable air ($m^3/h$) distributed through piping networks to seal habitats and greenhouses.
- Failure to maintain sufficient supply halts greenhouse production and disables suit refilling.

#### **Greenhouse**
- **Requirements:** Continuous oxygen and water supply.
- **Mechanics:** Features dedicated crop slots for sustainable food production.

#### **Research Center**
- An upgradeable internal machinery unit housed within the Main Building.
- Converts gathered geological samples and scientific studies into mission funds.
- **Upgrades:** Research speed and profit multipliers.

#### **Research Drill**
- Deployed on specific geological nodes to unearth valuable rock samples.
- **Stats:** Production time, sample rarity (determined by drill tier and location spot), and maximum inventory stock.
- **Logistics:** Limited stock capacity before manual retrieval; unused drills can be sold for 50% of their purchase price.

#### **Rover**
- **Stats:** Speed, food capacity, oxygen capacity, battery charge level, and charging speed.
- **States:** Stationary, moving, or solar-charging (activated by deploying solar panels).

#### **Solar Panels & Transformers**
- **Solar Panels:** Generate raw electrical power based on sunlight exposure.
- **Transformers:** Manage power distribution via physical cables connecting panels to transformers and transformers to individual base buildings. They also optimize crop growth rates.

#### **Crops & Agriculture**
- Features permanent plant strains that produce yields continuously every few minutes.
- Players can remove existing crops to swap strains, including Genetically Modified (GM) crops that offer higher or faster yields.

## 3. Systems & Gameplay Loops

### 3.1 Economy & Funding
- NASA provides a modest baseline budget hourly.
- Primary income is generated through scientific research—either by studying geological samples on-site or sending completed research reports back to Earth.
- Funds are reinvested into infrastructure expansion, facility upgrades, and supply procurement.

### 3.2 Survival & Life Support
- **Oxygen Supply:** Distributed via dedicated pipeline networks. If a habitat loses connection to an oxygen source, its internal reserves deplete hourly, shutting down connected greenhouses and trapping the player without suit refills.
- **Suit Autonomy:** The suit's oxygen tank refills automatically upon entering any pressurized, oxygen-supplied indoor environment. Depleting suit oxygen results in mission failure.

### 3.3 Infrastructure Logistics: Piping & Power
- **Pipes:** Transport oxygen and water across the base. Pipelines are built grid-by-grid with dynamic branching. Oxygen and water pipelines are strictly separated and visually distinct.
- **Cables:** Connect solar panels to transformers and power grid nodes to energize structures.

## 4. Progression & Achievement System
To guide players through the complexities of Martian colonization, **Make Mars Great Again** features an integrated **Achievement System**. Achievements serve as navigational milestones for the gameplay progression:
- **Milestone Tracking:** Clear objectives reward players for reaching key developmental stages (e.g., establishing the first automated water extraction, upgrading the main habitat, or hitting specific scientific research thresholds).
- **Structural Guidance:** By rewarding the successful scaling of life-support networks and agricultural self-sufficiency, achievements gently teach optimal base-building strategies without rigid tutorials.
- **Resource Incentives:** Completing specific milestones unlocks bonus mission funds or rare upgrade blueprints, driving engagement and rewarding strategic foresight.
